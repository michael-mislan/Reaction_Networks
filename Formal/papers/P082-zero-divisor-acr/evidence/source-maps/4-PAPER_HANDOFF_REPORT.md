## Mathematical result and proof path

The paper answers the original bimolecular necessity and sufficiency questions negatively, demonstrates the mixed-order failure, proves the corrected block-order coverage theorem, and preserves the lossless product-release result. The added results explain how a candidate can become a usable concentration guarantee:

1. **One regular positive point gives a sufficient coverage bridge.** For the original real ideal generated by s independent field combinations, full raw Jacobian rank s at one positive point gives a smooth real patch dense in its complex component. Global ACR makes the coordinate difference vanish on that component. Its descended minimal prime is associated to the original quotient, giving an actual nonzero annihilator class. The previously compiled block theorem then includes the ACR value. The argument neither replaces the original ideal by its radical nor infers ACR from local constancy. The contrapositive localizes missed ACR to rank-deficient positive geometry. The paper compares its quantifiers with source Proposition 3.27/Theorem 3.28 and the generic-geometry literature.
2. **A witness floor turns an identity into an error bound.** From `(x_i-alpha)^m h=sum q_j f_j`, a declared `|h|>=h0>0` yields the stated residual bound. Perturbations enter through the signed quantity `q·d`, preserving cancellation. For added fluxes `d=Mv`, `q^T M=0` is sufficient insulation. Equilibrium existence remains separate. The m=1, finite-sum, signed-load, integer-power and matrix-cancellation inequalities compile.
3. **The loaded reactor has exact capacity and conditioning boundaries.** Positive equilibrium exists precisely when Delta and N are positive, and is unique. The load leaves alpha unchanged but decreases the positive B/C pool. A prescribed witness floor gives a stricter load bound. The characteristic determinant and cubic inequalities compile. A self-contained complex-root calculation and variation-of-constants argument establish local nonlinear stability. Global attraction is not asserted. The conditional logarithmic time-average identity is included with its exact floor assumptions.
4. **EnvZ certificates are conditioned by available pools.** Both identities are connected to the literal nine-flux, seven-species field. The positive parameterization, conserved totals and scalar quadratic produce exactly one positive equilibrium in each total class with Xtot>0 and Ytot>alpha. Explicit interval pool floors quantify certificate gains. These are equilibrium floors, not automatic transient guarantees.
5. **Release preserves equilibrium algebra but has calculable costs.** Intermediate prefix residuals enter the transported certificate. At fixed flux, weighted storage equals `F sum omega/lambda`; a finite budget bounds throughput. Leakage produces a stage-dependent product yield and changes the old field by a known defect. The product-loss bound controls that defect; it does not extend the lossless quotient theorem to a changed system.
